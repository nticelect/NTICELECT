1 · How IBIT actually works

IBIT is a grantor trust, not a BlackRock investment decision. The chain has four links:

Investor (pension, RIA, retail)→ Authorized Participants create / redeem shares→ The trust buys / sells BTC→ Coinbase Custody holds the coins

The Authorized Participants — the firms that create and redeem IBIT shares in kind-size blocks — include JPMorgan Securities, Jane Street, Virtu Americas and Macquarie, with Goldman Sachs, Citadel Securities, Citigroup, UBS and ABN AMRO also named in filings. BlackRock's job in this machine is administration and brand. The directional risk belongs to the people at both ends of the chain — never to the house.

Cash vs in-kind. At launch in January 2024, creations were cash-only: an AP wired dollars, the trust bought the Bitcoin. On 29 July 2025 the SEC approved in-kind creations and redemptions (Release No. 2025-101): APs can now deliver Bitcoin directly. That matters for reading headlines — after that date, part of what flow trackers record as an “inflow” can be a migration of coins into the ETF wrapper, not a fresh spot purchase.

3 · Why the headline is wrong four times

“BlackRock bought” — no. End investors bought shares; APs created them; the trust acquired coins. BlackRock decided none of it.

“Billions in Bitcoin” — the dollar figure is share value at market price on creation day, and since in-kind approval, part of it may be coins changing wrappers, not new demand hitting the spot market.

“Bullish signal” — a creation can equally be one leg of a hedged trade. Through 2024, leveraged funds ran record CME shorts against long ETF exposure (the basis trade); as the basis compressed toward ~3% by August 2026 — below the ~3.8% T-note yield — those shorts unwound from roughly $14B to roughly $4.5B. Flow without the futures cross-check is half a fact.

“BlackRock's Bitcoin” — the 811,116 BTC belong to IBIT's shareholders. BlackRock's own money in Bitcoin funds is a rounding error: its house funds held on the order of $47M (Global Allocation) and $155.8M (Strategic Income Opportunities, 30 Sep 2025) — client money again, and under 0.5% of IBIT's assets.

6 · The headline checklist

Eight checks to run before any “BlackRock bought” headline moves you:

  1. Coins, not dollars. Convert the flow to BTC. A $500M inflow at $110K is not the same fact as at $60K.
  2. Whole complex, 5–10 sessions. One fund, one day is noise. Net the whole ETF complex over a week or two.
  3. Cross-check the basis. Compare flows with CME open interest and CFTC positioning: long ETF + short futures is a hedge, not conviction.
  4. Watch the premium and the spread. Persistent premium says demand is chasing shares; discount says the opposite.
  5. Respect the data lag. Flow tables publish after the close and vendors revise them. You are reading yesterday's weather.
  6. Apply the in-kind haircut. Since July 2025, some “inflows” are wrapper migrations with no spot buy behind them.
  7. 13F filings are a photograph, not a film. Quarterly, lagged up to 45 days, and they mix hedged and outright books.
  8. For Strategy, read the 8-K, not the tweet. Buys, sells and dividend funding are all in the filings — including the sells.
